iMac G5

10 of October 2025 - Gamerabbit


The iMac G5 came out the 31 August 2004, it had 3 revision during its lifespan. It is the first to introduce this white polycarbonate design, that was award winning and the 3rd iteration reused on the first generation intel iMac (2006). It was also the very influencing on the futur design of iMacs.


The Technical specifications of the iMac G5 are as follows:

  • Wi-Fi 802.11a/b/g/n
  • Frequency: Simultaneous 2.4GHz and 5GHz
  • Data Throughput: 450 Megabits per second (Mbps)
    • Interfaces:
    • 1x RJ-45 Connector for 10/100Mbps Ethernet WAN
    • 1x RJ-45 Connector for 10/100Mbps Ethernet LAN
    • USB 2.0 for Network Printing
    • 3.5mm headphone Jack with Mini-TOSLINK for AirPlay 1 and 2 output
    • Security:
    • WPA/WPA2
    • WPA/WPA2 Enterprise
  • MiMO: 2x2:2 per band
  • Support for up to 50 concurrent devices
  • CPU: Marvell 88F6183
  • Audio Codec: Texas Instruments Burr-Brown PCM2705 16bit or external DAC via Toslink
  • Flash Memory: 16MB
  • RAM: 64MB
  • Model Number: A1392
  • Dimensions : 98 mm x 98 mm x 22 mm


The last public release of MacOS for the iMac is MacOS Leopard, but there is also community made MacOS like MacOS Sorbet Leopard which was meant to improve speed and security and it even comes with it’s own AppStore, and there are also Linux Distribution for the PowerPC Macs (Like Adelie or Debian). But there is a less known MacOS Snow Leopard developer release, which is quite unstable after having some fun with it but some community developers are trying to patch it and improve it (macrumors MacOS Snow Leopard on PowerPC Thread).

The iMac G5 underwent three major revisions during its lifespan:

  • First and Original iMac G5 (August 2004):The debut model, designed to accommodate the power-hungry and heat-intensive G5 processor. Its internal layout was divided into three cooling zones: processor, hard drive, and motherboard/power supply. This design approach influenced all subsequent iMacs and remains evident today—though in a much thinner form—with the aluminum stand supporting both the display and internal components.

  • Ambient Light Sensor Revision (May 2005):This update introduced an ambient light sensor and an integrated AirPort Extreme Card (Wi-Fi), which was previously an optional add-on. The design retained the ease of access for installing or repairing internal components.

  • iSight iMac (October 2005):The final revision featured a built-in iSight webcam and Front Row, allowing infrared remote control. The ports were repositioned horizontally, and the overall design was carried over to the first Intel iMacs (Core Duo and Core 2 Duo). This version was discontinued just months later, making way for the transition to Intel-based models.


The iMac G5 was unveiled at the Apple Expo in Paris, succeeding the G4 iMac—nicknamed the "Sunflower iMac"—which was celebrated for its bold and innovative design. The first revision was particularly user-friendly, allowing easy installation of the AirPort Extreme Card (Wi-Fi) and simplifying internal repairs. The second revision added the ambient light sensor and integrated the Wi-Fi card, offering a modest but meaningful upgrade. The third and final revision represented the most significant update in the iMac G5 lineup. While maintaining the same form factor, it introduced the iSight camera, Front Row functionality (with an infrared sensor and remote), and horizontally aligned ports. This design remained visually consistent in the Intel iMacs until 2007, when Apple introduced the aluminum-and-plastic hybrid models.
